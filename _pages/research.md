**Thesis Abstract:**
<div style="margin-left: 2rem; margin-right: 2rem;">
This thesis sets out to provide a coherent synthesis of the various accounts of intellectual intuition that Schelling furnishes across the major junctures of his corpus. Its purpose is twofold. Firstly, to counter the charge of flippancy, or the belief that Schelling lacked philosophical rigour. While it is true that he provides many seemingly contradictory accounts of intellectual intuition throughout his writings, these contradictions can help reveal rather than obscure its place and purpose within his philosophy. By tracing the various contradictions surrounding intellectual intuition throughout Schelling’s writings I hope to demonstrate the existence of a continuous and unitary philosophical thread. The second purpose of this thesis is to clearly distinguish Schelling’s use of intellectual intuition from Kant’s and Fichte’s. Some significant work has already been done toward this end, but remains incomplete without a conception of intellectual intuition that can hold across Schelling’s entire corpus. The project starts with the period between 1775–1776, right at the beginning of Schelling’s philosophical career where signs of contradiction are already apparent between an original intellectual intuition responsible for positing all reality through itself, and a more quotidian intellectual intuition which attempts to reclaim experiential knowledge of that original act. I then attempt to map this dynamic onto the distinction between aesthetic and intellectual intuition from the 1800 System, and then also the tension present in the *Naturphilosophie* between a self-constructing nature and the philosopher’s attempts at scientific reconstruction. I consider Schelling’s first explicit attempts to resolve these contradictions in his *Identitätssystem* before moving on to discuss their persistence in his late “positive philosophy”. The thesis concludes with an evaluation of the possibility and relevance of a so-called “positive” intellectual intuition.
</div>
